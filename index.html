A bit about me

Background, research interests, and what drives me.

Hello! I'm Xiaowen Qi, a Ph.D. candidate in Electrical and Computer Engineering at the University of Maryland, College Park (expected December 2026), advised by Dr. Shuvra S. Bhattacharyya in the Maryland DSPCAD Research Group. I received my B.S. in Electrical Engineering from the University of Maryland, Summa Cum Laude.

My research sits at the intersection of edge and embedded ML, geospatial AI, and Explainable AI (XAI). I build systems that are not just accurate, but transparent, efficient, and deployable — from FPGA-deployable spatio-temporal models for additive-manufacturing monitoring to interpretable graph neural networks for urban crime forecasting.

I also enjoy bridging theory and practice: shipping research into usable tools, from PyQt geospatial analytics apps to the explainability and visualization tooling I built as an R&D Software Engineering Intern at Keysight Technologies in Summer 2026.

IEEE SMC 2026

PRISM: Disentangled Spatio-Temporal GNN for Interpretable Crime Forecasting

A white-box spatio-temporal graph neural network that pairs fixed spatial and contextual prior graphs with a residual-guided learned latent graph. Gated disentangled message passing, graph orthogonalization, and targeted regularization keep the three relation channels semantically distinct, yielding edge-level attribution for every prediction. Improves RMSE, MAPE, and R² over single-graph ST-GNN and ablation baselines across biweekly, monthly, and bi-monthly scales on 20 years of citywide crime data fused with census, land-use, housing, and business-location features.

ASME IDETC/CIE 2026

FPGA-Deployable Spatter Prediction for Additive Manufacturing

A staged 2D+1D CNN that fully decouples spatial and temporal processing for melt-pool spatter prediction, halving parameters (699K → 356K) at accuracy comparable to Conv3D and (2+1)D baselines. An operator-level compatibility analysis showed it is the only evaluated spatio-temporal architecture end-to-end compilable by FINN. Reusing spatial features across overlapping frame windows gives a 6.7× (CPU) / 5.7× (GPU) streaming speedup at ≤4 KB cache, and quantization-aware training (8/4/2/1-bit) compresses models up to 8× while retaining ≥85% of baseline accuracy.

Folium

Geospatial Analysis Platform for Crime Prediction

Two desktop applications that take you from raw public data and incident records to a trained, evaluated crime-forecasting model and a per-region risk ranking, all on a regular CPU. The Census / POI Alignment app re-distributes U.S. Census block-group statistics and points-of-interest onto custom regions (e.g. police beats) with area-weighted overlay and interactive maps; the Crime Prediction Wizard aggregates incidents, builds spatial and contextual neighbor and lag features, trains and compares models, and ranks regions by predicted risk.
